# Style: easy to read, impactful

Wilson reads these at night in Da Nang, often on a phone. The reading sections (1–3) must make sense in one pass. Precision lives in the Appendix.

## The 10 rules
1. **Lead with the "so what".** Every section and every item opens with one plain sentence: what happened and what it means for the book.
2. **At most 2 numbers per sentence** in Sections 1–3.
3. **Round in Sections 1–3:** 20%, not 20.26%; $680, not $676.20; 4.9%, not 4.891%; $350K, not $349,732.22. Exact figures go in the Appendix.
4. **Translate numbers into meaning:** "past your stop", "2% above your strike", "expires Friday", "$680 underwater". Not "Δ −0.43, cushion −3.2%".
5. **A number appears in Sections 1–3 only if it changes a decision.** VIX1D, VVIX, gamma levels and σ bands go to the Appendix unless they trigger something.
6. **One status marker per item:** 🔴 act today · 🟡 watch · 🟢 fine / working. These are the only emoji allowed.
7. **Same slots, same order, every report** (see `report-layout.md`).
8. **Small tables in Sections 1–3:** at most 4 columns and 8 rows. Big tables go to the Appendix.
9. **No links, rule tags or bracket tags in Sections 1–3.** Write the plain idea ("concentration limit", not "R5"); the rule number goes in the Action table's rule column only. Sources go in the Appendix.
10. **Short sentences, active verbs, no filler** ("staged a broad-based relief rally amid…" → "Stocks rallied because…").

## Item template (Section 2)
> 🟡 **Headline: what happened, in plain words.** One or two key numbers.
> **Why:** the cause, with its tag (earnings, oil, rates…). If no source confirms it: `cause unconfirmed`.
> **Your exposure:** the positions it touches and what changed (cushion, $ at risk).
> **So what:** act / watch / fine, in one sentence.
> **Watch:** the level or date that would change the call.
